## Project Overview

### Project Title

Multi-Agent Substrates: Observing System Dynamics over Self-Evolving Graphs to Enable Pre-Behavioral Interventions

### Project Tier

Tier 2

### Project Duration (months)

36 months

### Plain-language Summary

AI systems are increasingly deployed not as isolated chatbots but as populations of agents that read from and write back into a shared medium — the agent substrate of memory, context, retrieval, and tool state, where each write reshapes what every other agent retrieves on the next turn. The proposed lab will study whether this substrate carries early, measurable signals of coordination health and emerging coordination failure — signals that precede any individual agent’s behavioral expression of failure — and whether reversible structural interventions on the medium can restore healthy coordination without overriding individual agents. The result would be a class of oversight that scales with the size and depth of multi-agent deployments instead of being outpaced by them.

### Keywords

multi-agent coordination; pre-behavioral observability; amplified oversight; coordination phase dynamics; substrate instrumentation

### Key Scientific Question(s) & Agenda Fit

Every multi-agent AI system is governed by structural scaffolds — prompts, behavioral policies, retrieval capabilities, and tool access that surround each individual agent and govern what it can do. Around a single agent, this scaffold, a “harness”, is a closed engineering object: someone designs it, deploys it, and it stays put. What we call a **“substrate”**, by extension, is the shared medium through which *multiple* agents communicate, aggregate information, and coordinate. Each write into the substrate is retrieved by other agents on subsequent turns, so a single write can propagate through the population. 

The substrate has been invisible as a measurement target. Figure 1.a makes this concrete. We compare a topographic static printed map versus Google Earth view as an analogy. Current behavioral oversight is like the 2-dimensional static map. Resultant artifacts come after the fact and are static at creation. The substrate approach is the progressive zoom view: able to carry early signals before any individual agent's output expresses failure (Figure 1.a). Figure 1.b shows the shared medium — a typed, composable frame graph through which agents read and write back, with a membrane shaping what each agent retrieves next without overriding any individual agent. Figure 1.d shows intervention: two co-activating attractors forming before behavioral failure is visible; the membrane operator fires; healthy divergence/convergence is restored.

**Key scientific question:** Does the multi-agent substrate 1\. carry early measurable signals of coordination health that precede any individual agent's behavioral expression of failure, 2\. generalize across populations, and 3\. ground a new class of reversible structural interventions? Four sub-questions structure the inquiry. 

1. **Q1 — Observability:** What substrate signals detect stuck states earlier than post-hoc behavioral evaluation?   
2. **Q2 — Formalism:** Which formal language — dynamical-systems, active inference, FEP, sheaf-theory, oscillator order-parameter — best characterizes coordination phase?   
3. **Q3 — Intervention:** Can reversible structural interventions restore healthy cycling without overriding agents?   
4. **Q4 — Depth:** Does the substrate signal remain informative as orchestration hierarchies deepen? 

This proposal targets Aim 3.2 (multi-agent collective dynamics), §3.1b (amplified oversight as deployment-time control), and §2.1 (construct and predictive validity) of the Schmidt Research Agenda.

### Problem and Impact

AI increasingly mediates information dissemination, increasing attack surfaces. Foundational infrastructures like this shape what will be possible in the future. We propose an alternative to post-behavioral detection mechanisms, describe an ‘early warning system’ using a substrate-level measurement framework, and offer a medium in which future detection mechanisms can be described, compared, and validated.

The feedback dynamics that ensue once agents write back into the shared substrate distinguish a substrate from a static deployment scaffold. What one agent writes, other agents retrieve, and a single write can cascade through the population. This marks a shift from discrete query/response to continuous exchange among always-on agents in which the substrate evolves with every write, potentially including rules that govern what each agent can do.

Most current observability is trace-based, reconstructed from outputs and logs after the fact. It remains useful as forensics, but does not scale with the size and depth of multi-agent deployments, and by the time a problem from a single agent becomes legible, other agents might already have retrieved and acted on faulty contributions. 

We anchor against coordination medium pathology risks:  

* *premature collective convergence* (agents collapse onto a narrow frame before completing exploration);   
* runaway tool or compute use (agents loop, over-query, over-plan; resources deplete without progress);   
* *substrate contamination* (adversarial content propagates through shared memory and retrieval);   
* *multi-agent failure propagation* (a local error is retrieved as evidence and iteratively amplified); and   
* *role-inappropriate dynamics* (a divergence/convergence pattern healthy for one role may be pathological for another — trustworthiness depends on matching substrate profile to the stakes of the task).

The supervising principal — human, or amplified oversight mechanism — sits outside the system, reading outputs and inferring upstream causes. Information asymmetry scales with deployment and heightens the principal’s interpretation burden – until verification is no longer tractable.

**Assumption**

The substrate carries early, measurable signals of coordination health and emerging breakdown. Reversible structural interventions can restore healthy coordination without overriding individual agents. Both claims will be tested with construct- and predictive-validity standards.

**What we propose**

The substrate is built as four layers: a self-evolving graph of typed, recursively composable frames; a measurement layer that exposes phase dynamics across the graph; a coordination layer where membranes form around agents and their co-active context; and an intervention layer of reversible, region-scoped operators on those membranes.

On this medium, the §3.2 measurement targets — properties arising through interaction, collective failure modes when no single agent is misaligned, propagation of local failures through networks — appear as field properties accessible without inspecting any individual agent’s internals. The principal-agent gap inverts: the medium becomes legible at the level the agents operate on and normative judgment stays with the principal. 

**What this enables**

*Best-case:* An intervention protocol that restores healthy cycling without agent-level override. *Minimum valuable:* a falsifiable answer to which signals precede which failures, at which lead time.

**Timeliness**

Multi-agent ensembles are becoming the default architecture. Behavioral oversight is already structurally insufficient: collective failure propagates faster through a shared medium than current evaluation can reconstruct it. 

### Approach

The proposed work establishes a research lab studying continuous dynamic effects within multi-human-multi-agent environments. The instrumentation is built to the extent needed to function as a research apparatus — to perturb substrate conditions, observe trajectories, measure cycles, detect contamination, test interventions. Outputs are testbeds, metrics, protocols, and validated intervention patterns.

Testbed architecture will be instrumented as follows:

The **graph layer** is the substrate’s representational ground; it holds typed, recursively composable semantic frames as units. An agent, in this architecture, is itself a configuration of frames — prompt, tools, retrieval policy, accumulated state all live in the graph. Context is what constitutes operational agency; agents, semantic units, and human participants differ in scale and dynamism, not in kind. Frames compose recursively — a session contains frames but is itself a frame; a territory contains frames but is itself a frame. This compositional uniformity is what makes the substrate fractal in the technical sense: same dynamics at every scale of nesting. It is also the precondition for the Q4 depth analysis.

The **measurement layer** observes the graph’s phase dynamics; resonance — patterns of phase alignment between configurations of context — is the quantitative target. Coordination health, drift, and lock-in appear as field properties rather than statistical patterns over independent observations. Candidate formalisms for the measurement layer are three families: spectral / sheaf-theoretic (Laplacian eigenvalues; cohomological obstruction measures across orchestration boundaries; cellular-sheaf discretizations with sensor-fusion precedent); active inference / Free Energy Principle (healthy cycling as generative-model update vs. contraction; nested Markov blankets for membrane structure); adaptive networks of phase oscillators (Aoki & Aoyagi 2009; Berner et al. 2023). Conventional graph/message-passing observables serve as comparator baselines. The proposal commits to the category of methods, not specific instruments.

The **coordination layer** is the structural layer where membranes emerge, a coupling boundary region in the substrate of densely co-activated context surrounded by sparser coupling. Membranes form over a population of agents by tracking which context is co-activated and acted on: frames retrieved together accumulate coupling (Hebbian: retrieved together, wired together), repeated co-retrieval establishes a temporary boundary, absence of co-retrieval lets it dissolve. With that we are establishing *folds* in contrast to *folders*. Flows gate by category: discrete, syntactic, identity-bound, and by flow rate: continuous, structural, dynamic. Membranes are observed and characterized at the coordination layer; access policies belong to a different layer.

The **intervention layer** acts on those membranes. The substrate is the fast-changing constitutive layer; agent behavior is the slow-changing operational layer. Each run, the substrate equips its agents with context — instructions, retrieved frames, tool state, harness state. Agent behavior is a downstream effect of context composition. This makes the moment of equipping the natural leverage point: substrate-level observability is by construction pre-behavioral, reading the structure that will shape the next behavior before behavior expresses. The lever we propose is a region-scoped operator that modulates the density, rate, and direction of provenanced information flow across a membrane. Membranes do not override agents; they shape the context each agent receives on the next turn by changing the permeability of the medium itself. Raising coupling promotes convergence; lowering it creates room for divergence. Quarantining a substrate region holds contaminated context out of the next retrieval; restoring it lets the region back in. Asymmetric membranes let information flow one way but not the other. Behavioral oversight catches behavior after the fact; substrate oversight via membranes catches the conditions that produced it, and crosses orchestration boundaries that behavioral evaluation cannot.

**Conceptual foundations**

The concept synthesizes several traditions: frames and frame semantics — knowledge as structured, composable units (Minsky 1975; Fillmore 1982); stigmergy — coordination through traces in a shared medium rather than explicit messaging (Heylighen 2016; Goss et al. 1989; Tero et al. 2010); coupled dynamical systems — collective behavior as synchronization, phase transition, lock-in, drift (Kuramoto 1984; Strogatz 2000); field-based models of distributed cognition (Atasoy et al. 2016); cybernetics — regulation through feedback, requisite variety, chosen leverage points (Ashby 1956; Meadows 1999).

**Workstreams**

Four overlapping workstreams structure the work. Each has explicit falsification conditions; a negative result at any stage is a publishable contribution that bounds the field.

**Q1 — Observability** (months 1–12). Pre-register candidate substrate signals across a 5-regime stress registry: accumulation pressure (context growth driving attractor closure), context poisoning (adversarial content propagated through retrieval), topic-trap construction (substrate seeded for circular reasoning), forced fragmentation (coupling attenuated across regions), and unaccountable-enclave construction (substrate regions where coordinated behavior cannot be attributed to an authenticated principal). Construct-validity comes from human annotators trained on a pre-registered failure taxonomy. Acceptance criterion (uniform): a substrate signal is accepted only if it precedes annotator-labeled failure by a pre-registered lead-time threshold, survives permutation null, and outperforms trace/log and graph baselines on earlier detection across ≥2 populations.

**Q2 — Formalism** (months 6–18). In parallel with Q1, implement and compare the candidate formal instruments on the live substrate. Test whether ≤5 order parameters discriminate four coordination phase states (exploration, stabilization, lock-in, drift). M18 decision point: which formalism(s) best characterize coordination phase; whether discrete-sheaf formulations of Markov-blanket structure hold under empirical test. The decision informs Q3 — membrane variables are chosen based on which order parameters carry the predictive signal.

**Q3 — Intervention** (months 13–30). Build the membrane operator at the coordination/intervention layers. Pre-registered membrane variables: permeability, directionality, coupling density, flow rate, attenuation/amplification, provenance retention, quarantine/reconnection, revocation, and human-checkpoint thresholds. Every intervention is reversible, logged, and surfaces to the principal in real time through a navigable read-and-act surface — the substrate’s living knowledge graph exposed as semantic structure, not raw telemetry. Safety envelope: verifiable attribution to authenticated principals is preserved throughout; the substrate continues to surface its phase to the principal at all times during intervention.

**Q4 — Depth** (months 24–36). Characterize how Q1 signals and Q2 order parameters degrade across orchestration depth. The substrate’s compositional uniformity (frames inside frames at every level) is the architectural precondition that makes this measurable; whether the dynamic behavior of signals remains informative across depth is what Q4 tests. Identify the fidelity floor and what it implies for governable hierarchy depth.

### Novelty

**Related work**

Multi-agent safety work (Bo Li, Dawn Song, Zilberstein) measures agent behavior, not what is happening in the coordination substrate. Coupled-oscillator and dynamical-systems formalism (Kuramoto 1984; Atasoy et al. 2016; Aoki & Aoyagi 2009\) provides candidate mathematical language, but no AI coordination substrate has been deployed to compute these observables. 

Known subproblems include: 

* prompt injection and context poisoning as adversarial writes into shared substrate state (Greshake et al. 2023\)  
* memory contamination as substrate-mediated failure under accumulating context;  
* Diversity-collapse research in LLMs (Song et al. 2025; Li et al. 2025\) documents convergence costs at training time, not at the coordination layer.   
* Schmidt's 2025 cohort includes adjacent work on Improving the Legibility of Inference-Time Reasoning for Scalable Oversight; this proposal extends the legibility concern from individual-agent reasoning to the medium through which agents coordinate.

**The contribution**

A legible medium without required inspection of model internals. Coordination state surfaces as a continuously observable field property, readable before behavioral failure expresses — formally characterized, accessible through orchestration depth, and legible to the principal before behavioral failure expresses. **To date, this multi-agent substrate has not been treated as a first-class measurement target.**

1. *Substrate-level pre-behavioral observability.* Pattern detection at the medium agents coordinate through, tested in Q1.   
2. *A new class of safety lever — structural intervention upstream of behavior.* Acting on the coupling structure of the medium gives a different guarantee profile than behavioral oversight and reduces the principal's information asymmetry while preserving normative authority.  
3. *A candidate unified formalism for coordination dynamics.* The boundary objects of the active-inference / FEP family (Markov blankets) appear to sit as a special case within the more general formalism of sheaf restriction structure. Whether discrete-sheaf formulations (cellular sheaves; precedent in sensor-fusion studies) capture coordination dynamics on a live AI substrate is what Q2 tests.

It implements provenance-anchored typed-frame semantics and several Q1 candidate observables are already computable on the existing schema: frame-diversity indices, phase-coherence measures across active regions, and trace-cluster recurrence patterns. The instrumentation needed to operationalize Q2's candidate formalisms is buildable on the schema and constitutes first-year work. The Q3 membrane operator does not yet exist; building and characterizing it is the Year-2 work this proposal funds. A technical brief on substrate readiness is available to the review panel on request.

**Team capacity**

The lab is led by three co-PIs whose research programs intersect on the substrate-level coordination question, with three additional researchers covering complementary work streams.

**Open empirical questions**

Whether substrate signals precede behavioral failure. Whether any specific candidate formalism is central. Whether the FEP↔sheaf equivalence holds on discrete substrates. Whether substrate dynamics show scale-invariant behavior across orchestration depth. All four are empirical questions the funded work tests.

### Tier 2 Ambition

Substrate-level observability cannot be established at a small scale. The core scientific claims require testing across multiple model families, agent architectures, and population sizes and configurations.  A lone testbed with a single model family would produce findings too narrow to be scientifically credible or safety-relevant. The stress regimes, model families, and cross-population generalization studies that give Q1 its construct-validity argument require sustained instrumentation capacity that a Tier 1 project cannot support.

The membrane operator in Q3 requires a fully instrumented substrate before intervention experiments can run. Building the measurement layer, validating signals, formalizing order parameters, and only then testing interventions is a sequential dependency chain that spans 30 months by design. Compressing it produces either premature intervention claims or unvalidated signals. Neither one is acceptable for a safety-relevant result.

The human evaluation program — IRB-cleared, 300+ participants, mixed human-to-agent ratio experiments — cannot be meaningfully conducted at a smaller scale. The ratio question in particular requires sufficient population size to detect phase-dynamic shifts as human participation varies. This is not a study that can be piloted in Tier 1 and extended; the statistical requirements set a floor on sample size from the start.

Why confidence is warranted: RAGE exists as a live instrumented substrate with provenance-anchored typed-frame semantics. Several Q1 candidate observables are computable today. The team spans the disciplines the problem requires: dynamical systems, active inference, sociotechnical systems design, distributed software infrastructure. Prior work from this interdisciplinary team reaches deep into each of the four research question intersections. The failure modes this project targets are already documented empirically (Cemri et al., NeurIPS 2025: inter-agent misalignment accounts for 36.9% of failures across 1,642 annotated traces). The science is not speculative; the instrumentation to study it at the required scale is what this funding makes possible.

### Proposal Risks

Every falsification in this proposal has non-null scientific value. Each negative result rules out a candidate tool, establishes a hard constraint on what governance architecture can do, or identifies a missing formalism. The field learns something consequential regardless of which direction each question resolves.

**R1 — Substrate signals don't precede behavioral failure.** The principal research risk and the primary falsification target. Pre-registered stress regimes across multiple populations give it multiple chances to surface cleanly. A clean negative establishes that collective AI failure has no pre-behavioral signature — that oversight is structurally post-hoc, not temporarily limited. That finding constrains what the field should invest in next.

**R2 — Signals don't generalize across populations.** Observables valid in AI-only hierarchical coordination may not transfer to mixed human-AI settings. A bounded failure characterizes the scope of substrate observability and identifies which coordination configurations require alternative oversight mechanisms.

**R3 — Intervention produces safety-relevant side effects.** Attribution to authenticated principals must be preserved across all Q3 interventions. Attribution-chain breakage is a project-blocking finding — and establishes that structural substrate intervention is incompatible with the accountability requirements of safe multi-agent deployment.

**R4 — Candidate formalisms fail to deliver.** If none of the candidate instruments (spectral / sheaf, active inference / FEP, adaptive-oscillator) yields a small set of generalizable order parameters, the result establishes that coordination phase requires new mathematics and characterizes where the existing formalisms break. The FEP↔sheaf equivalence holding only on continuous substrates would be one specific instance — and a meaningful result in its own right.

**R5 — Scope creeps into interpretability.** The substrate is the medium between agents, not the inside of any model. Agents are treated throughout as black-box producers and consumers of substrate state. Nothing in the proposal reaches inside model internals — the interpretability boundary is architectural, not just procedural.
