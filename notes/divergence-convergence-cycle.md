Healthy work over a substrate *cycles*. Divergence opens the space: new paths are explored, distant regions are encountered, and the range of active material widens. Convergence closes the space around something usable: relationships stabilize, a direction gathers support, and the population becomes able to act.

<Figure id="divergence-convergence" margin caption="The working field opens, meets constraints, and gathers around a workable direction." />

Neither movement is healthy on its own. What matters is whether it fits the task and its stakes. A routine task may need to converge quickly over a narrow context. An open research question may need to range widely before settling. The same concentration can be useful in one setting and premature in another.

Across interaction trajectories, this movement appears as exploration, stabilization, lock-in, and drift:

- **Exploration** expands the active field and brings alternatives into contact.
- **Stabilization** retains relationships that are proving useful.
- **Lock-in** closes the field before alternatives have had a meaningful chance to compete.
- **Drift** continues producing activity without enough consolidation to support action.

The failure modes belong to the cycle. Premature convergence turns a useful attractor into lock-in: the substrate keeps returning the population to the same region and makes alternatives harder to encounter. Failure to converge leaves the population moving without accumulating a workable configuration.

<Figure id="belief-attractors" margin caption="A lock-in pattern: one region repeatedly draws activity back toward itself until alternatives become difficult to reach." />

The Observatory makes these movements visible as they happen. The research question is which sequences reliably distinguish productive convergence from lock-in, or productive exploration from drift, early enough to support a reversible intervention.
