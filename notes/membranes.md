A *membrane* is a computed, temporary boundary around a co-active region of a substrate. It is detected from the interaction structure that has formed there, rather than assigned in advance as a team, topic, or permission group.

<Figure id="substrate-hero" margin caption="Temporary boundaries become visible where activity gathers and fade as that activity releases." />

Membranes make a collective state easier to read. Instead of inspecting every actor or knowledge unit separately, we can observe the region they are currently working through together: its [[movement]], what it repeatedly encounters, and how it relates to its surroundings.

Co-attention is not agreement. A membrane says that material and actors are consequentially active together; it does not say that they share a belief, goal, or interpretation. Conflict can be as structurally important as alignment.

## Overlap and scale

Membranes overlap and nest rather than partitioning the substrate into fixed clusters. One actor's context can sit within a project region while also drawing from another region; a boundary meaningful at one scale may disappear at another.

<Figure id="membrane-nesting" margin caption="Co-active regions can overlap and nest, preserving more than one useful boundary at once." />

## A place to intervene

Because a membrane describes the boundary of available context, it also provides a place for reversible intervention. A system can change whether material beyond the boundary is admitted and how much detail is disclosed, then observe how the collective dynamics respond.

<Figure id="membrane-permeability" margin caption="A boundary can mediate admission and resolution without prescribing an actor's response." />

This shifts oversight from issuing instructions to every participant toward shaping the shared conditions under which they coordinate.
